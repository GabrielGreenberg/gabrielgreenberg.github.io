Mental Iconicity

About the seminar

This seminar will examine the question of format in mental representation, from the perspective of an informational and computational theory of mind. Taking the language of thought hypothesis as our foil, we will look at evidence and arguments for the existence of diagrammatic, pictorial, and map-like representations in the mind and in the brain.

Requirements

Short paper
5 page short discussion paper. Due Fri 2/17. In about 5 pages, summarize and evaluate a single idea (or two closely related ideas) from one (or two) of the readings. Discuss at least one example in detail.
Long paper preview
Meeting with the professor to discuss the longer term paper. Due Mon 2/27: by this date, schedule a meeting with me to discuss themes or an outline for your final paper.
Long paper
10-15 page long term paper. Due Fri 3/24. In 10-15 pages, discuss an issue that is deals with, or is related to, the themes of this course. Prioritize depth over breadth. Detailed examples are essential. Illustrations are recommended. The long paper may build upon the short paper.
Weekly question prompts
Each week prepare 2-3 questions about the reading to share with the class.
Readings
I have generally assigned two readings per week, and to get the most of this class, you should do both readings. In the interest of good classroom discussion, I have starred (*) one reading each week which the discussion will focus on. You really must read this one!
